SEC-02 DOES IT REACH YOU?

Article 3 is short, and it is the whole applicability question. Any one of three triggers is enough, and two of them catch companies with no EU presence at all.
3(1)
EstablishmentYou have an EU office or branch.
3(2)(a)
OfferingYou offer goods or services to people in the EU.
3(2)(b)
MonitoringYou track the behaviour of people in the EU.
No EU office required. That is exactly how a Singapore business gets pulled in.
The two numbers that focus the mind

72 hours to tell a regulator after you become aware of a breach. 4 percent of global turnover as the ceiling on a fine. Everything else on this sheet is work done so those two numbers never arrive as a surprise.

8 rightsArt 12-22

  • be informed (13/14)
  • access (15)
  • rectification (16)
  • erasure (17)
  • restriction (18)
  • portability (20)
  • object (21)
  • automated decisions (22)
Accountability is the one that changes everything: Article 5(2) says it is not enough to comply, you must be able to demonstrate that you comply.
The mindset shift for a PDPA-trained leader

The six bases are co-equal doors, not a hierarchy with consent at the top. Consent is one door of six, and often the flimsiest. For most business uses contract or legitimate interests fits better, and does not evaporate when someone changes their mind.

Most of the eight rights have no equivalent under the PDPA. If your process only does access and correction, it was built for the wrong regime.

SEC-06 WHERE SINGAPORE TEAMS GET CAUGHT

"It is Europe's problem"The most common mistake a Singapore leader makes. Ask: do we offer to, or track, anyone in the EU?
Consent for everythingThe weakest and most fragile of the six doors, chosen by default. Ask: would contract or legitimate interests fit this purpose better?
A notice that hides the basisIt lists cookies, but not the lawful basis or the retention period. Ask: could a reader tell why we hold this, and for how long?
Access and correction onlyA request process built for the PDPA meets maybe two of eight rights. Ask: what happens when someone asks for erasure or portability?
No adequacy decisionSingapore has none, so every transfer out needs a mechanism, and since Schrems II an assessment too. Ask: which mechanism covers this flow?
Starting the clock lateThe 72 hours run from when you become aware, not from when the investigation finishes. Ask: who can start the clock, at 2am?
The insight that makes running both regimes manageable

On almost every point one regime is stricter than the other. Build to the stricter of the two and one operation satisfies both the GDPR and the PDPA, which is the whole argument of session b8.
